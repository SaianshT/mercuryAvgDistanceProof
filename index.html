Mercury Is the Closest Planet

Counter-intuitively, Mercury is the nearest planet on average to every other planet in the solar system — because it hugs the Sun and never strays far, while other planets spend long stretches on the opposite side of their orbits.

How It Works
The simulation models each planet orbiting the Sun at its real relative speed (simplified as circular orbits). At every time-step, the distance between the reference planet and every other planet is measured and added to a running average. Mercury's orbit is just 0.39 AU — so it's always near the Sun, and therefore always reasonably close to every planet. Venus or Mars may get closer at times, but they also swing to the far side of the Sun, driving up their averages.
