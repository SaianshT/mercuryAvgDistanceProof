Why Is Mercury the Closest Planet to Earth?

Surprisingly, Mercury is the nearest planet on average to every other planet — because it never strays far from the Sun, while distant planets spend long stretches on the far side of their orbits.

How it works: Each planet orbits the Sun at a fixed radius (simplified as circles). The simulation samples the distance between the reference planet and every other planet at each time-step, building a running average. Because Mercury's orbit is tiny (0.39 AU), it stays near the Sun — and therefore near every planet — most of the time. Larger-orbit planets like Venus or Mars swing far away when on the opposite side of the Sun, inflating their averages despite being sometimes closer.
